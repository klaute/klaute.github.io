Anonymized Engineering Case Studies

How complex technical situations became robust solutions.

Projects and clients remain anonymous for confidentiality reasons. The examples show way of working, responsibility and impact without disclosing confidential technical details.

Reusable Embedded Security Platform

Initial situation

Across several automotive projects, embedded systems had to integrate security functionality. HSM integration, cryptographic services and security integration paths became recurring technical tasks.

Challenge

When each project interprets these topics again, coordination effort rises, integration patterns diverge and technical risks become harder to plan. The solution had to be reusable across projects without assuming that all projects were identical.

Role and approach

The work focused on building and evolving a reusable embedded security platform. This included standardized integration approaches, integration of HSM and cryptographic services, and technical guidance for using platform structures across several projects.

Impact

In this context, security integration effort was reduced by approximately 75 percent. More important than the number was the chain of effects: reuse, less repeated coordination, simpler integration of additional software modules, better planning and more efficient long-term engineering through a platform approach. Further development was technically owned across more than 15 projects per year.

What this example shows

Platform Security is not only a protection concept. When structured properly, it becomes a reusable platform capability that connects architecture, integration and engineering enablement.

Turning Engineering Knowledge into an Enablement System

Initial situation

Technical knowledge around Platform Security and Security Engineering was embedded in project experience, recurring workflows and many detailed decisions. New engineers had to understand this knowledge before they could work independently.

Challenge

Simple handovers or occasional explanations are not enough in such situations. Knowledge has to become findable, understandable and durable without turning engineering enablement into a formal training product.

Role and approach

Since 2021, technical knowledge was captured from the beginning in internal wiki articles, documents and how-tos. Recurring technical workflows were also documented in explanatory videos. Security Engineering Insights made security topics transparent for others. This was complemented by implementation concepts, technical guidance and review structures.

Impact

The individual artifacts gradually became an onboarding and enablement system. Around 8 to 10 engineers were enabled to work independently in Platform Security or Security Engineering. Knowledge was no longer tied only to individual people.

What this example shows

Engineering Enablement works when architecture decisions, how-tos, reviews and knowledge transfer become part of the technical solution. The goal is not more documentation, but teams that can work.

Local AI-Assisted Engineering Knowledge System

Initial situation

The starting point was the departure of an experienced expert and the risk that important engineering knowledge would become hard to find or use. Later, personal knowledge was also prepared so others could use it after the creator had left.

Challenge

This was not a general AI topic. Confidential documents, source code and internal knowledge had to remain inside controlled boundaries. At the same time, existing knowledge had to become findable, analyzable and traceably usable.

Role and approach

A local RAG-based system was developed. Local processing, access protection, traceability and security by design were architecture requirements. AI was used as a technical component to make engineering knowledge structurally accessible.

Impact

The system made knowledge and recurring support partially available and reduced personal dependencies. It did not replace a person; it helped keep existing engineering experience usable in a controlled way.

What this example shows

AI-assisted engineering becomes robust when treated as a Systems Architecture and Platform Security task. AI is the tool, not the business purpose.

Making Extensive Security Requirements Decidable

Initial situation

Projects had to analyze extensive sets of security requirements, in some cases roughly 300 requirements. Project management and engineering teams needed a clear view of which requirements were relevant, feasible or still open.

Challenge

Large requirement sets can quickly create false precision. The important part is not only collecting requirements, but making their technical contribution, open assumptions, exclusions, risks and affected components visible.

Role and approach

The requirements were assessed structurally and translated into needed software components, platform capabilities, effort, risks, assumptions and exclusions. The results were prepared transparently for project management and the involved engineering teams.

Impact

Technical decisions became more traceable. Other disciplines could derive which contributions were needed in their components. Systems Architecture was therefore not treated as greenfield design, but as clarification of complex existing dependencies.

What this example shows

Robust architecture work often happens where requirements, existing systems, multiple teams and open decisions need to be brought into a shared technical view.

Cross-Project Technical Coordination and Effort Transparency

Initial situation

Numerous projects needed support at the same time. Technical questions connected project management, development teams, security owners and other specialist functions.

Challenge

Cross-project technical support only works when assessments are robust and traceable. Effort estimates need to show scope, assumptions, risks and exclusions without implying budget or personnel responsibility.

Role and approach

The work consisted of technical coordination, effort estimates and preparing decision foundations. The goal was to bring different technical roles into a shared view and structure open points so decisions became possible.

Impact

Transparent, robust and goal-oriented assessments built trust in the technical evaluation. As reputation increased, technical assessments were accepted as decision support. The reference to more than 15 projects per year is used only to describe the cross-project context.

What this example shows

Technical judgment is visible not only in designing individual systems, but also in structuring complex project situations calmly, traceably and decisively.
